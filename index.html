[06]how a rule firescomputed from the real orbit, every engine tick

When it moves, your coin moves.

A coin on orbt carries a rulebook: up to six lines of when its satellite does something → the coin does something. The engine computes the satellite’s position from its latest element set every few minutes and fires a rule once per real event: one lap, one sunrise, one pass over a city. [09]the engine

One wallet per coin. Every action public.

The engine is custodial, and says so. Each coin gets its own wallet, derived on the server from one master secret, and that wallet is the coin’s pump.fun creator. The engine claims the creator fees into it, reads the satellite, and when a rule fires it spends exactly what the rulebook says. Every action is a Solana transaction linked from the coin’s page.

the engine does

  • launch the coin on pump.fun from your deposit
  • send your first buy and unused SOL back to you
  • claim creator fees into the coin’s chest
  • compute the satellite’s orbit from its latest elements
  • buy back and burn, airdrop or pay holders when a rule fires

the engine cannot

  • sell the coin (there is no sell action)
  • withdraw the chest to anyone not named in the rulebook
  • change a rulebook after launch (its fingerprint is in the coin’s description)
  • fire a rule twice for the same lap, pass or sunrise
  • guess: if the orbit can’t be computed, the rule waits

what it costs

  • pump.fun’s own fees, paid from your deposit
  • a 0.015 SOL reserve stays in the coin wallet for gas
  • orbt takes 5% of each fee claim, only when a studio wallet is set (checking…)

where the data comes from

  • orbits: CelesTrak public element sets, refreshed every 3 hours
  • positions: SGP4, the standard propagator for these elements
  • the sun: the astronomical almanac’s low-precision formula
  • markets: Dexscreener, then the pump.fun curve itself

[10]questions

Straight answers.

Are these real satellites?

Yes. Every dot is an active satellite from CelesTrak’s public catalogue of element sets, the same data amateur trackers and many operators use. Positions are computed with SGP4, the propagator those elements are made for. Altitude on the globe is compressed so everything fits on one screen.

How accurate is the tracking?

For low satellites, typically within a few kilometres when the elements are fresh, drifting as they age. The catalogue refreshes every three hours, and each satellite page shows how old its element set is.

What does a coin have to do with its satellite?

The coin is named on its satellite and its rulebook watches that satellite’s real orbit. orbt has no link to the satellite’s owner or operator, and owning the coin doesn’t mean owning anything in space.

Can two coins be launched on the same satellite?

No. One coin per satellite, first come. A launch that is waiting for its deposit holds the satellite for 45 minutes, then lets it go.

What is a pairing?

A public link between an existing pump.fun coin and a satellite, recorded on orbt. It is free and needs no wallet; nothing happens on chain. The coin shows on its satellite on the live sky and on the satellite’s page with its live market from Jupiter. A coin can be paired with one satellite; a satellite can carry many paired coins. Rulebooks only run on coins launched on orbt, because the engine can only spend fees it holds.

Who holds the coin’s fees?

The coin’s own wallet, run by the engine. It is custodial and the page says so everywhere. The engine can only spend the chest the way the rulebook says: buy back and burn, airdrop to holders, pay holders in SOL, or pay the creator.

What happens when the satellite re-enters?

Once the public catalogue records its decay, re-entry rules fire, once. After that the satellite is gone from the sky and from the live globe, and the coin carries on as a coin.
